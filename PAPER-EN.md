## Abstract

The article is devoted to one of the urgent problems in the field of studying and organizing youth policy: the assessment and formation of the value of patriotism. Current political rhetoric often speaks of a record high level of patriotism in Russian society, including among students, but with a closer study of the social environment, we have to admit that such statements are somewhat declarative. In this regard, the purpose of this article was to determine the content of the concept of patriotism as a value, which formed the basis of the methodology of the author's sociological research devoted to the study of the formation of social values among students. The survey was conducted from May 17 to June 1, 2023 through the personal offices of full-time RANEPA students (N=4338). Based on the results of factor and cluster analysis of the array of sociological data, a factor of the value of patriotism was formed, as well as typological groups of respondents described depending on the distribution of coordinates of cluster centers and the analysis of estimates of judgments that make up the content of the factor of patriotism and additional, clarifying judgments. The typological group of respondents in the zone of positive values of the factor is called "social humanists"; they highly value judgments concerning patriotism and are ready to develop and support their country in every possible way; they believe that the well-being of each person is inextricably linked to the well-being of society. The typological group of respondents who are in the zone of negative values of the factor (three clusters) are called "social individualists"; they have a negative attitude to patriotism as a social value and are not ready to share the fruits of their labor with their country, do not consider themselves to owe anything to it. The third typological group, traditionally intermediate, is called "socially undecided"; its representatives are in the zone of neutral, zero values of the factor and do not demonstrate a pronounced positive or negative attitude to the value of patriotism. In order to determine organizational and methodological approaches to the formation of the value of patriotism, according to the results of the correlation analysis, a list of values that are related to the value of patriotism was formed, of which half are evaluated sharply negatively by "social individualists", and half are evaluated positively by them. It is assumed that the development of mechanisms for the implementation of youth policy aimed at increasing the level of patriotism, based on positively assessed values, will be more effective in terms of forming patriotism among students who have a negative attitude towards it. This constitutes the author's next research task.

## Introduction. Substantiation of the Problem

According to the results of a survey conducted in 2024 by VCIOM,[^1] 94% of Russians call themselves patriots. Moreover, the share of those who consider themselves unconditional patriots has grown over the past year by 10%, setting a new all-time high (62%). At first glance, this thesis, which of course evokes positive emotions, does not exhibit any contradictions or difficulties in interpreting the results. However, upon closer examination it becomes obvious that a rather broad approach is taken to assessing the level of patriotism, one that requires clarification with regard to the content of the concept, since, in our view, it is quite expansive and encompasses various indicators. In this connection, it seems not entirely correct to assert a high level of patriotism on the basis of assessing only a single judgment rather than an aggregate of features.

Confirmation of this thesis is seen in the results of two sociological studies conducted five years apart, which present us with a collective image of the emotional foundation of patriotism — a "complex of positive feelings associated with the perception of contemporary Russia" [1, p. 304] which, in the opinion of M. K. Gorshkov, act as one of the fundamental bases of patriotism (see Table 1).

The first thing that draws attention in Table 1 is a noticeable tendency toward a decline among respondents in two of the three positive feelings (love: 10.5% in 2020 versus 17% in 2015; pride: 16.4 and 24%, respectively; only the level of respect grew over the five years by two percentage points: 28 and 26%, respectively) against the backdrop of an almost twofold increase over five years in indifference (8.9 and 5%, respectively) and negative emotions (resentment/shame: 15.4 and 7%, respectively; indignation: 12.5 and 6%, respectively). At the same time, high negative figures cannot necessarily be an aggregate indicator of unpatriotic sentiments in society, since objective love for the Motherland represents the ability to see not only its merits but also its shortcomings [2], constituting the content of "transformative" (in the terms of I. A. Khaliy) [3], "constructive" (in the terms of the authorial collective: R. T. Schatz, E. Staub, H. Lavine) [4], and "civic" (in the terms of I. M. Kuznetsov) [5] patriotism.

Of course, in this particular case we cannot present the entire range of reasons for such a comprehensive decline in the positive perception of their country by the respondents of the studies indicated, since we do not have the whole array of data at our disposal; moreover, we do not set ourselves such a task. At this stage we only want to draw attention to the fact that, given the seemingly generally high level of patriotism recorded in the previously cited VCIOM study, a detailed examination of the state of patriotism in Russian society reveals, on the whole, a not-so-positive emotional disposition among the majority of respondents toward their country, jointly determined by the assessments of two groups of indicators of emotional attitude, but not yet reflecting the full content of the concept of patriotism.

This fact underscores the need to clarify the concept of patriotism both in terms of its content and in terms of measuring the level of patriotism in individual age groups. First and foremost, in the group of student youth, which represents, on the one hand, the potential basis for the socio-economic growth and development of our country and, on the other hand, a socio-demographic group with as-yet-unsettled values and therefore susceptible to both positive and negative influences from various agents of influence.

Despite the unquestionable relevance of the demand for determining the level of patriotism in the youth environment, there are insufficient studies devoted to this question to give an unambiguous answer to the question of the real level of patriotism and the factors that determine it.

Thus, according to the results of an empirical study of the student youth of Moscow universities, conducted in May 2017 by a group of staff members of the Faculty of Sociology of Lomonosov Moscow State University under the leadership of N. G. Osipova and S. O. Elishev, 84.2% of respondents "have a positive attitude toward patriotism," 5.4% "a negative" one, and 10.4% "found it difficult to answer" [6, p. 120–121]. However, interpreting the presented results, one may allow that a respondent, in expressing his opinion about patriotism as a social phenomenon, did not necessarily consider it in application to himself and did not intend by this answer to affirm that he himself is a patriot. Moreover, such figures by no means indicate that, having a positive attitude toward patriotism as a social phenomenon, respondents consider themselves patriots, while understanding by patriotism something of their own.

The results of a more detailed analysis of the outcomes of the VCIOM study[^4] cited at the beginning of this article also point to the need for a detailed study of the factors influencing the level of patriotism in different age groups, first and foremost in the youth environment. We made use of the array of data posted in open access on the study's page and constructed a diagram based on a contingency table that includes answers to the question "How would you characterize yourself — as a patriot of your country or not?" and age, which we grouped ourselves, distinguishing within the socio-demographic group of youth (14–35 years) two age subgroups according to the principle of the age of integration into the educational process: 18–24 years[^5] — most often students of secondary vocational schools or universities; 25–35 years — young people who have completed a full cycle of vocational education and have fully entered the labor market.

Figure 1 shows that representatives of the younger groups are distinguished by a lower level of patriotism than representatives of the adult groups. Thus, "unconditional patriots" are fewest in the very youngest group (44.7%), only slightly more numerous in the second "young group" — 51.1%; at the same time, among representatives of the adult, pre-retirement, and retirement groups the share of "unconditional" patriots is considerably greater (61.1; 68 and 68.6%, respectively). Proceeding from such a distribution of "unconditional patriots" across different age groups, it is not surprising that there are more "rather patriots" in the young groups (42.6 and 43.1%, respectively) than in the adult ones (34.7; 25.6 and 26.2%, respectively).

Thus, one can say that the youngest group of respondents is in the zone of "patriotic risk"; it is precisely in this group that the largest number of respondents demonstrating a tendency toward unpatriotic self-perception is found: 10.6% of respondents aged 18–24 consider themselves rather "not patriots" versus 1.73% on average among respondents of the other groups who are inclined toward a rather unpatriotic disposition. This fact is of great interest to us, both scientifically and administratively-instrumentally, since there arises a need to identify and describe in more detail the characteristics of the group of youth that is not distinguished by a high level of patriotism, and to develop organizational measures for its reasonable correction.

## Patriotism as a Value: Operationalization of the Concept

Patriotism, being a complex and multifaceted social phenomenon, has for many centuries been the subject of reflection by a wide circle of people, including scholars, politicians, cultural figures, and, of course, ordinary laypersons. Despite the fact that in the traditional understanding patriotism most often includes love for one's country, its culture, history, and values, in the scholarly literature we encounter different approaches to both the functional and the substantive description of this concept, which correspondingly determine also the essential terms underlying the formulations. Thus, for example, according to the majority of domestic dictionaries, patriotism is a certain positive emotion, a feeling — devotion and love for the Motherland;[^7] in the Federal project "Patriotic Education," implemented within the framework of the national project "Education,"[^8] patriotism is described as an "acquired quality" which, reflecting an upbringing-related character, "is a subject of transmission… and constitutes a most important component of the individual's worldview." However, in our view patriotism represents not merely a positive emotion or a transmitted quality, but a social value that is being formed.

When we speak of patriotism not merely as a certain positive emotion or social phenomenon, but as a value, we assume that it represents, in a certain sense, an ideal behind which stands a collective, social principle (É. Durkheim [7, p. 300]); such an aggregate of principles as acts as a regulator of actions and is realized through the acceptance of obligations within the framework of a system of the desired type (T. Parsons [8]); a really existing good that is a product of the historical development of culture (H. Rickert [9]).

The content of the concept of patriotism, like that of value, is not static; one cannot say that it has not taken shape in scholarly terms, yet over the course of each epoch it has been adjusted under the influence of a constantly changing social reality, which is of great interest to us. Therefore, in the course of elaborating this concept, we allow ourselves to rely not only on scholarly research but also on the notions of the opinion leaders of their time, in order to have a broader picture of the description of patriotism as a social value, as "a certain temporally stable, socially significant universal that: defines a person's attitude toward social reality; characterizes the potential readiness to follow these universals; includes an aggregate of actions reflecting adherence to these universals; acts as an individual or group guideline of social activity, simultaneously prompting it and being its social regulator" [10].

Thus, for example, in L. N. Tolstoy's article "Patriotism and Government" (1900), we encounter the judgment that "true, good patriotism consists in wishing one's own people or state real goods, such as do not violate the goods of other peoples."[^9] However, Lev Nikolaevich goes on to speak of the harmfulness of patriotism as an instrument for manipulating the consciousness of citizens and one conducive to the unleashing of military action, holding that "patriotism, as a feeling, is a bad and harmful feeling; as a doctrine, it is a stupid doctrine, since it is clear that if every people and state considers itself the best of peoples and states, they will all be in a crude and harmful delusion."[^10] Lev Nikolaevich, as a participant in the Crimean War, deeply agonized over the necessity of the participation in wars of people who were initially removed from politics, first and foremost peasants, whose principal task was working the land, which constituted, in L. N. Tolstoy's view, their calling. Therefore, such an understanding of the content of patriotism is grounded in a series of wars into which the Russian Empire was successively drawn and which, as we recall, did not have as their key aim the defense of one's own country but answered to the narrow, often egoistic tasks of politicians (a "small victorious war"). Of course, in such a connotation the concept of patriotism does indeed evoke not the most positive emotions and, to some extent, even resonates in content with the concept of nationalism.

We encounter a rather similar position among colleagues from Stony Brook University, who see in patriotism "a form of collective identity in which someone promotes the needs and/or desires of the nation at the expense of individual egoistic interests," which presupposes the inseparability of patriotism from the collective interests of the country and those political processes that inevitably involve the individual in the implementation of their decisions, proceeding from "the best interests of the nation" [11]. From the context of the article we see that this position of the scholars is again connected with war: "If the ability of the U.S. government to wage war in Iraq is not sufficient proof of the continuing viability of patriotism as a collective identity, the ongoing catastrophe in the Balkans, the tension between Pakistan and India, and the seemingly endless number of international hot spots attest to its enormous appeal." In the end, the authors conclude that the guarantee of ensuring a civilized and peaceful organization of the world order is "the need to strengthen the forces of antipatriotism."

An emphatically opposite point of view can be seen in the Report of I. V. Stalin at the ceremonial meeting of the Moscow Soviet of Working People's Deputies with the party and public organizations of the city of Moscow on November 6, 1944, which speaks, among other things, of the role of "ardent and life-giving Soviet patriotism" in the victory over the fascist invaders in the Great Patriotic War. The Report emphasized that the strength of Soviet patriotism lies in the fact that "it has as its basis not racial or nationalistic prejudices, but the profound devotion and loyalty of the people to their Soviet Motherland,"[^11] as well as an interethnic unity possessing the power to unite all the nations and nationalities of the country. From this context it is obvious that adherence to the value of patriotism is one of the key factors in strengthening and consolidating Russian society before a common enemy. Here we see that in adherence to the value of patriotism there is manifested its function of unification, of ensuring interethnic unity, which today, alongside patriotism, is defined as a traditional Russian spiritual and moral value.[^12] It is obvious that, manifesting themselves simultaneously, such values reinforce one another; therefore one of the research tasks of the empirical part of our study is to determine the values related to patriotism with the aim of its additional reinforcement.

In the PRC such values were adopted at the 18th Congress of the Communist Party of China. Perhaps by virtue of the continuity of socialist ideology, patriotism, alongside devotion to one's cause, honesty, and kindness, is one of the socialist values of the PRC. Colleagues from Peking University note that patriotism is "a spiritual support, a guide to action, a norm of behavior for establishing relations between the person and the country" [12, p. 165], adherence to which will inevitably lead to the attainment of the "Chinese dream of realizing spiritual strength." In this formulation, a value-based approach to defining the concept of patriotism is clearly traced.

It should be noted that, despite the fairly unambiguous and in places even radical approaches to the organization of social space that took shape in the course of the evolution of Chinese society against the backdrop of the somewhat ambiguous historical events of the 20th century, Chinese colleagues note that a person's lack of citizenship is not the main criterion by which one can define a person as not a patriot. Thus, Chinese colleagues believe that "as long as a person cares for and loves the people of his homeland, cares about the development of the country, has a grateful heart toward the country and patriotic ambitions, even if he has changed his nationality, he still remains a patriot, and we cannot deny his feeling of patriotism" [12, p. 164]. Let us note that such a description of the social portrait of a patriot very closely characterizes a whole series of Russian émigrés who ideologically could not accept the change in the political course of our country at the beginning of the 20th century, but who until the last days of their lives remained true patriots of their country. Thus, for example, widely known are the charitable gestures of transferring funds by the great Russian composer and pianist Sergei Rachmaninoff from his concerts during the years of the Great Patriotic War to the Red Army fund and to the Defense Fund.

At the same time, contemporary Russian public opinion diverges in its attitude toward the attributes of patriotism from the opinion of Chinese colleagues. Thus, according to the results of a survey conducted by VCIOM[^13] in 2020 and devoted to the study of the patriotic sentiments of Russians (see Table 2), more than half of respondents (64%) believe that a person who wants to move to another country cannot be a patriot; at the same time, respondents' opinions were distributed in almost equal shares regarding the inclusion or non-inclusion among patriots of those citizens who have a second citizenship (50% not a patriot, 42% a patriot); who work in a non-commercial organization designated a "foreign agent" (47% not a patriot, 40% a patriot); or who refuse to participate in elections (46% each "for" and "against" inclusion among patriots).

The key attribute of a person's being a patriot, according to the opinion of the absolute majority of respondents, is love for Russia (85%), correlated with patriotism in, perhaps, all the key dictionaries, and therefore not causing great surprise. It is telling that in second place in this peculiar ranking of the attributes of patriotism stands not such a graphic attribute as, seemingly, would separate a citizen from the country and could become an indicator of an unstable patriotic position — such as, for example, the aforementioned prospect of departure to another country — but rather ignorance of the state symbols of Russia (59%), having an account abroad (57%), and having a second citizenship (50%). Noticeably more respondents associate unpatriotic behavior with actions inflicting direct or indirect damage, harm on their country: bribery (80%), evading army service (65%) and taxes (64%).

Let us note that, if a person wants to marry a foreigner, he may perfectly well be a patriot in the opinion of 72% of respondents, since the creation of a family is a personal matter for everyone that will not have a negative effect on the well-being of the country (with, perhaps, rare exceptions); moreover, even the public expression of an opinion not coinciding with the point of view of the state, in the opinion of the majority of respondents (68%), is not a principal attribute of non-patriotism. Thus, the key attribute of the manifestation of patriotism is the principle "do no harm to your country," as well as "love your country," ensuring its prosperity and socio-economic stability.

Thus, the content of the concept of patriotism as a value must be disclosed proceeding from the fact that patriotism, like value in the classical understanding, constitutes "deeply rooted beliefs that guide people toward the world in which they live" [13, p. 222–230]; these are "social patterns" surrounding a person from his first to his last days, "a certain 'social field' that determines his deeds, his activity, his attitude toward the world" [14, p. 7], not depending "in the eyes of the members of society on the individual desires of each of them" (italics by the author, André Corten) [15, p. 18]; such deeds may manifest themselves in a special attachment to one's country, in self-identification with the country, in readiness to sacrifice oneself and one's interests for the good of the country, as well as in a special concern for the well-being of the country [16, p. 34–35].

Proceeding from the conducted analysis of scholarly sources that characterize patriotism from various angles, one can formulate the value functions of patriotism, which are also its indicators:

– the consolidation of society: patriotism promotes the creation of a sense of community and unity among the country's citizens, helping to overcome national differences and conflicts, uniting people around common values and goals;

– the strengthening of national identity: patriotism forms a sense of belonging to the nation, strengthens its identity and historical memory, helping people to understand their role in society and to feel themselves part of something larger;

– protection from external threats: patriotism can unite the nation in the face of an external threat and contribute to its defense, making it possible to mobilize the resources and forces of the nation to repel enemies;

– the strengthening of statehood: patriotism helps to strengthen the state, creating the conditions for its stability and prosperity, stimulating citizens to respect and observe the laws and traditions of the country;

– motivation for public activity: patriotism can be a powerful stimulus for people to participate actively in public life, to contribute to the development of the country, and to solve public problems.

Hence, patriotism as a social value is seen by us as a temporally stable, socially significant universal manifested in love for one's Motherland, adherence to which ensures the strengthening of national identity and statehood, motivation for public activity, and the consolidation of society, including in the face of external threats.

## Empirical Basis and Research Methods

The operational definition of the concept of patriotism given above is one of the elements of the methodology of the sociological diagnostics of values, at the basis of which lies the author's empirical model for the analysis of value [see: 10; 17], which includes the assessment of four groups of indicators of each value under study:

– the assessment of the ideal value relation to social reality within the framework of each value under study;

– the assessment of the significance of each indicator included in the description of a specific value;

– the assessment of the readiness to perform actions corresponding to each value under study;

– the assessment of the reflection of adherence to the value in concrete actions and deeds (action).

The list of values under study was formed on the basis of a comparison of traditional Russian spiritual and moral values,[^14] as well as of values which, according to the methodical recommendations developed on the basis of the Ministry of Science and Higher Education of the Russian Federation,[^15] have strategic significance and should underlie the implementation of youth policy at the university level.

Such an approach determined the structure and content of the sociological instrument — the questionnaire used in the course of the survey of representatives of student youth, the results of which constituted the empirical basis for the preparation of this article. The questionnaire included four blocks of value judgments characterizing the elements of each value under study: a block of judgments about the ideal attitude toward the value; a block about the "significance" of the value; a block about readiness to follow the value; and a block about the action performed in accordance with this value. Let us note that each of the indicators proposed in this article for the study of patriotism as a value corresponds to one of the elements of value assessment that we have formulated.

In the questionnaire, agreement or disagreement with the judgments was proposed to be assessed on a nine-point Likert scale, which was chosen in connection with the fact that the analysis of the sociological data obtained presupposed the use of, among other things, methods of multivariate statistical analysis — factor and cluster analysis.

For the purpose of testing the author's methodology of the sociological diagnostics of values, from May 17 to June 1, 2023, the author conducted an online sociological study, "Traditional and Strategic Values of Student Youth,"[^16] among full-time RANEPA students (N=4338) through their personal offices, of whom 89% are students in bachelor's degree programs and 11% are students in master's degree programs, evenly distributed across years of study; 64.5% are female and 35.5% male, in proportion to the composition of RANEPA students by sex.

In addition, the structure of the sample includes the distribution of respondents by place of study: students of the RANEPA regional network (59.4%) and students of the Moscow campus (40.1%). Of course, this sample does not represent all the student youth of Russia and characterizes only the students of one specific university; however, it reflects its regional specificity, since it was composed in proportion to the number of RANEPA students studying in the branches of specific regions. ## Patriotism in the Context of Traditional Russian Values

One of our research tasks is to determine the values related to patriotism, which constitutes not only a scientific but also an organizational-instrumental interest. The point is that today in our country a great many attempts are being undertaken to form and ensure the stability of the value of patriotism in the youth environment through the implementation of a whole series of programs and projects of various levels: from university-level to federal. However, as we were able to ascertain earlier, the real level of patriotism in the youth environment is far from the desired declared one. It seems that in the course of developing new programs to raise the level of patriotism, it is important to take into account those values that are related to it and at the same time are accepted by the greater part of youth, including social individualists and the socially undecided.

In the previous section we have already described several values related to patriotism. However, on the basis of the results of factor analysis it is impossible to identify the complete list of these values, since the use of this statistical method solves other tasks; therefore let us turn to the results of correlation analysis.

We calculated the correlation of one of the key judgments about the value of patriotism ("It is important to me to be a patriot, to love my Motherland") with all the value judgments assessed within the framework of this study. As a result of correlation analysis by C. Spearman's method, six value judgments were distinguished that maximally correlate with the value judgment about patriotism (see Table 9); the p-level of significance of the correlation of all the judgments is the same: p < 0.001.

According to the obtained results of the correlation analysis, respondents associate with the meaning of the value of patriotism most of all the judgment characterizing the value of service to the Fatherland and responsibility for its fate (0.791), as well as two more values with high correlation coefficients: the value of historical memory (0.773) and the value of the continuity of generations (0.755). In addition, the list of related values included three values with medium correlation coefficients: the value of the rights and freedoms of the citizen (0.589), the value of the family (0.550), and the value of creative labor (0.535).

## Conclusion

Patriotism, being a very broad concept in substantive terms, has been examined in the article from the position of a value-based approach. In the author's understanding, it represents a temporally stable, socially significant universal manifested in love for one's Motherland, adherence to which ensures the strengthening of national identity and statehood, motivation for public activity, and the consolidation of society, including in the face of external threats. As the basis of this definition of the concept of patriotism as a social value, we designated for ourselves its key functional indicators: the consolidation of society, the strengthening of national identity; protection from external threats; the strengthening of statehood; motivation for public activity.

The indicated functional indicators constituted the content of the factor of the value of patriotism, on the basis of which typological groups were determined depending on their coordinates in the space of the factor: "social humanists" (the typological group in the zone of positive values of the factor of the value of patriotism); the "socially undecided" (the typological group in the zone of neutral values of the factor); "social individualists" (three typological groups in the zone of negative values of the factor).

Such an approach gave us the opportunity to understand that the representatives of the typological groups located in the zone of negative values of this factor diverge fundamentally from the representatives of the positive typological group in the assessments of judgments characterizing both the value of patriotism itself and the values related to it. They have a negative attitude toward all the judgments in the register of importance and readiness regarding the ensuring of the continuity of generations and the unity of the peoples of Russia, and the preservation of the historical memory of the people; they are not ready to work for the good of their country and its people.

At the same time, the representatives of both groups assess almost equally highly the values that determine the conditions which influence the comfort and success of their individual existence: professional development and self-realization, health, independence, dignity, justice.

Such results give us the opportunity to say that the greater part of the representatives of each typological group is oriented, first and foremost, toward professional development and self-realization. At the same time, the representatives of the typological group of "social individualists" are not ready to share with their country the fruits of what will constitute their professionalism, which is being formed thanks to the resources of this country — for example, inclusion in the system of higher education. The representatives of the opposing typological group, the "social humanists," on the contrary, in their social plans and orientations demonstrate confidence that the happiness and well-being of each person are inextricably linked with the well-being of the whole of society.

At the same time, "social individualists," having a sharply negative attitude toward half of the values we determined, on the basis of the results of the correlation analysis, to be related to patriotism (historical memory, continuity of generations, creative labor), assess very highly the second half of the related values: service to the Fatherland and responsibility for its fate, the rights and freedoms of the citizen, the family. In such answers there can be discerned, albeit an incomplete, nevertheless a partial manifestation of civic-mindedness, which demonstrates the potential for forming the value of patriotism among the "socially undecided" respondents as well, and among the "social individualists," through the implementation of youth-policy measures based on positively perceived values.
